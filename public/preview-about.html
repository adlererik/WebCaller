WebCaller security

WebCaller is an independent Signal-like browser phone. It uses official libsignal 0.105.1 through a custom WebAssembly/storage adapter. The core is pinned at 1738ead8a12ffe539f6309958123dadb16dc3b95 with unmodified protocol source. The browser integration and custom call verification remain unaudited; this is not an official Signal client.

Conversation encryption

New messages, attachment-key manifests and call signaling use PQXDH v4 (X25519 + Kyber-1024) and Double Ratchet plus SPQR/Triple Ratchet (ML-KEM-768). Ratcheted payloads use the pinned upstream AES-256-CBC, HKDF-SHA-256 and HMAC-SHA-256 construction with its 8-byte message MAC. Application envelopes and prekey publications have Ed25519 signatures. Existing P-256 identity anchors can certify an Ed25519 signer while preserving their fingerprint. Files and wrapped local records use AES-256-GCM with 128-bit tags.

Live audio/video uses browser-negotiated DTLS-SRTP, separate from the message ratchet. Post-quantum message protection does not make live media, classical identity authentication or the SAS post-quantum. Forward secrecy limits exposure of previously recorded traffic after used keys are discarded; retained readable history/file keys and a still-compromised phone are separate exposures.

Verify your connection

Compare all four words at the top of a call, in order. A fresh committed X25519/HKDF/HMAC exchange binds them to the accepted media fingerprints and identities; no Signal root secret is exported. Words are local interface text, not transmitted video pixels. Skipping comparison never blocks calling, but gives no extra human verification. In a chat, tap the contact's name/header to compare the full grouped 256-bit pairwise safety fingerprint through a separate trusted channel. A mismatch needs investigation.

The maintainer deliberately chose not to depend on third-party key-transparency services/auditors. Trusted invitations, persistent pins and manual checks do not provide automatic global directory-consistency verification.

Limits

The server still sees routing/timing/size/contact metadata and supplies executable website code. A malicious update, same-origin script or compromised phone can use keys and read plaintext. Nonextractable keys are not protection from a compromised usable origin. Disappearing messages/deletion cannot erase screenshots, downloads, recipient copies, OS/browser history or backups. No anonymity, forced ringing or independent security certification is claimed.

Includes MIT WebCaller code, Apache-2.0 Vodozemac SAS and AGPLv3-only libsignal. Download the matching corresponding source; private configuration and conversation data are excluded.
