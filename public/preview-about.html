WebCaller Test

This is an isolated experimental installation. Your usual WebCaller is separate. Use disposable accounts and messages here.

Engine: official libsignal 0.105.1 Rust core, pinned at commit 1738ead8a12ffe539f6309958123dadb16dc3b95, through a small experimental WebAssembly/storage adapter. The official protocol source is unchanged. This is not an official Signal browser client.

New messages, attachment-key manifests and call signaling use Signal session encryption established with PQXDH. The current library also exchanges SPQR/Triple Ratchet data. Application envelopes and prekey publications use Ed25519 signatures. New accounts have Ed25519 signing identities. Existing test accounts keep their original P-256 identity pins and authorize a nonextractable Ed25519 signing key with a P-256 certificate; their identities, sessions and history are preserved. Historical P-256 signatures remain readable. Refresh both test phones after this update. Files remain AES-256-GCM. Live audio/video remains WebRTC DTLS-SRTP: this experiment does not make live call media post-quantum secure.

The optional four security words still bind the accepted call media and authenticated application identities. Their existing Vodozemac SAS primitive is retained separately; message encryption no longer uses Olm. Read all four words to each other. Skipping the check does not stop a call.

Contact IDs, invitations, calling codes, timers, messages, media, alerts and call adaptation keep their normal workflow. Old Olm accounts/sessions are intentionally not imported. Your test identity/session state is kept locally so refreshing can be tested. No change here clears your normal site's keys.

Try it on your phones

  1. Set up the first test phone with the private test setup link/QR. Use a different Home Screen icon from your normal WebCaller.
  2. Invite the other test phone. On iPhone follow the invitation's Add to Home Screen instructions and open its new WC Test icon.
  3. Send messages both ways, refresh, reopen the chat and check that the conversation and contact remain.
  4. Call in both directions. Compare the four words, switch camera/voice/video, and check the displayed codec and rates.
  5. Send a photo during the call. Then test on different networks and walk between Wi-Fi/mobile data. Some browser/OS restrictions remain.
  6. Enable test-site browser alerts and, optionally, its separate ntfy subscription. Do not disconnect your normal WebCaller subscription. Verify the locked-phone sound yourself.

Tests establish interoperability and reliability, not a proof of security. This new browser adapter has not had an independent audit. The website still supplies executable JavaScript, and an owned phone can expose plaintext. Large PQ key/session records and the extra module may increase startup and storage work; physical-phone performance is part of this trial.

Includes MIT WebCaller code, Apache-2.0 Vodozemac SAS and AGPLv3-only libsignal. Download the corresponding experimental source (no private configuration or test data).
